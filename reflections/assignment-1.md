# Assignment 1 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was an analogy, not a piece of code: thinking about a
mechanical watch — how looking inside it reveals the mechanism behind
something you normally only see the face of — got me thinking about the human
body the same way, and specifically about the heart, which is exactly that
kind of hidden mechanism doing something continuous and easy to take for
granted. That's where the idea for the site came from.

The project itself also had a turning point: originally I'd only built what's
now webpage 3, the scrollytelling blood-flow explainer. Feedback from my
convenor pushed me to expand it into the full three-page site — a landing
page and a heart cross-section explorer ahead of it — instead of leaving the
explainer to stand alone without the anatomical grounding those earlier pages
give a reader first.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

A few things clicked. Batch prompts covering several changes at once turned
out to be much harder to walk back than specific, narrow ones, so I've moved
toward asking for one change at a time even when it's slower. Organizing
reference images and screenshots up front, before prompting, made everything
downstream faster — prep work isn't overhead, it's most of the leverage.
More broadly, the actual skill turned out to be deciding the logic of a
thing precisely enough to explain it; the same request worded differently
gets different results, so the thinking is the work, not the typing. That
also means the more I understand the underlying domain — video editing,
computer graphics, image processing — the better I can direct an AI toward
what I actually want, rather than approximating it.
